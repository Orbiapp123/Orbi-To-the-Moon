Brand Identity

Why "Orbi: To the Moon"

Orbi derives from the Latin orbis — meaning circle, world, or sphere. It evokes the Moon, orbital motion, and the cosmic world of the game. It's short, memorable, and domain-friendly.

"To the Moon" is one of the most culturally loaded phrases in crypto. From Dogecoin to Bitcoin ATH celebrations, it represents optimism, momentum, and community belief in a project. By making it part of our name, we're not following the trend — we're owning it.

Together, the name tells a complete story: a cosmic world, a Moon character, a community going up together. Every part of the game — the Moon character, the $STR token (Stars), the cosmic UI, the cheese (moon cheese) — ties back to the same universe.

And crucially, we want players to feel like their $STR token is going to the moon — that by playing, staking, and holding, they are part of something that is genuinely rising. The name isn't just aesthetic. It's a promise built into every mechanic.

App Aesthetic

How Orbi should feel

We want players to feel like they're actually on the Moon — a sense of depth, weightlessness, and cosmic wonder in every screen.

🌌

Dark Cosmic UI

Deep space backgrounds. Soft particle effects. Cards with subtle purple borders and a soft inner glow. Nothing harsh — everything glows softly like distant stars.

✨

Rarity = Visual Hierarchy

Common items are silver-grey. Rare items glow blue. Epic pulse purple. Cosmic items shimmer with rainbow gradients and animated borders. Rarity is visible immediately.

🎮

Chunky, Tactile Controls

Large tap targets. Animated visual feedback on button presses and item openings. Reward animations (coins, stars bursting) on every earn event — every action feels responsive and satisfying.

🌕

The Moon Character

The Moon is the central aim — the destination players are trying to reach. By making it your character, you feel like you are literally going 'to the moon'. It's not just a mascot; it's the journey itself. Non-transferable, so it becomes truly yours.

🧀

Whimsical Details

The moon eats cheese. Cows live on the farm. Seeds grow wheat in space. The absurdity is intentional — it's charming and deeply shareable. When someone posts "I just made cheese on the moon to feed my lunar cow", that's an organic viral moment no ad budget can buy.

📊

Financial Clarity

Token balances, staking yields, and marketplace prices are displayed cleanly and prominently. Players always know their position. Trust comes from transparency.

Meme Culture

Why memes matter more than ads

In crypto, culture travels faster than capital. The most successful projects didn't just build a product — they built a language.

The precedent is already set

Dogecoin became a multi-billion dollar asset on the back of a meme. Not technology — a meme. pump.fun turned meme coin creation into a cultural event, generating hundreds of millions in volume. "To the Moon" is one of the most recognised phrases in internet culture — and it's in our name.

Orbi doesn't chase meme culture. It's built from it. Every design decision — the Moon character, the cheese, the space cows — is shareable by design.

Why absurdist humour works

The best crypto memes are the ones that make you stop scrolling. "I'm farming cheese on the moon to feed my space cow" is the kind of sentence that gets screenshotted, quote-tweeted, and shared with friends. That sentence describes Orbi's actual gameplay — and it's inherently viral. No marketing budget required.

Crypto Twitter as distribution

Crypto Twitter (X) operates on a completely different attention economy to mainstream social media. A well-timed meme from a 500-follower account can reach 100,000 people in 6 hours if it resonates with the right community. Orbi's content strategy is built around creating those moments: leaderboard callouts, chest unboxing reactions, farm milestone posts — all designed to be shared.

"To the Moon" as a rallying cry

Every time someone posts about crypto gains, they reference the moon. Every time a token pumps, the community says "we're going to the moon." We've embedded that phrase into our identity — so whenever anyone says it, they're saying our name. We want players to feel that their $STR is genuinely going to the moon. The name becomes a self-fulfilling energy.

What makes Orbi content shareable

▸ Unexpected earning moments ("I made $X playing a phone game on my commute")
▸ Chest unboxing — the surprise of rarity is inherently watchable
▸ Leaderboard callouts — community recognition drives competitive sharing
▸ The absurdist premise — moon cheese, space cows — gets quoted and laughed at (positively)
